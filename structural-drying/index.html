Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Rochester Restoration Squad are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Rochester

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.
