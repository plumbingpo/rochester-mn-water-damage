Steps to Take When a Pipe Bursts in Rochester, MN

Home / Blog / Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in Rochester, MN

Few plumbing problems rattle a homeowner like a pipe that suddenly bursts. At 4 to 8 gallons every minute, one broken pipe can soak whole rooms, damage what you own, and compromise the structure in a matter of hours. Knowing exactly what to do in the first critical minutes can save you thousands of dollars in damage and separate a manageable repair from a major restoration project. Below, Rochester Restoration Squad lays out a step by step plan that homeowners in Rochester, MN can follow when it happens.

Step 1: Stop the Water at the Main Valve

The single most important thing you can do when a pipe bursts is stop the flow of water as fast as possible. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Step one is tracking down the valve that controls your main water line. Look by the water meter first, since that is where Rochester houses tend to have it, often in a utility room, garage, or basement, or on a street-side exterior wall. To stop water throughout the house, twist the handle clockwise (righty-tighty) until it stops. When the broken pipe is easy to spot and has a dedicated valve close by, shut that one too.

If you cannot locate or operate your shutoff valve, call your water utility immediately, since they can shut off water at the meter. This is one of the most important reasons to know your shutoff valve location BEFORE an emergency. Take a few minutes today to locate yours and make sure it operates smoothly. A valve that will not move should be repaired by a plumber now, not during a flood.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

Before the crew arrives, you can cut your losses, starting with getting documents, electronics, and valuables out of the wet zone. Furniture resting on damp carpet should be raised, with foil or plastic blocks set under each leg. Remove area rugs from wet floors. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Open closet and cabinet doors to promote air circulation. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker box, but only when that is safe and you are not standing in water.

Never walk through standing water near electrical outlets, appliances, or panels. Can't get to the breaker panel safely because of serious flooding? Leave the power as it is until trained help arrives and assesses things. Safety always comes first.

Step 4: Record the Damage for Your Claim

Your insurance claim will need evidence, so record everything carefully before any cleaning starts. Take photos and video of all standing water, damaged materials, and affected belongings. If the spot where the pipe failed is visible, record it, along with the day and approximate hour you first noticed the problem. Rochester Restoration Squad supplies detailed reports as well, and paired with your notes they strengthen your claim and your case for fair payment.

Preventing Burst Pipes in Rochester

Insulating bare pipes in crawl spaces, garages, attics, and other unheated areas is a big step Rochester homeowners can take, even though no measure prevents every burst. When temperatures drop, let faucets drip a little so water keeps moving in the lines. For sinks on outside walls, leave the cabinet doors beneath them open so warm air gets to the plumbing. Monitor your water pressure and install a pressure reducing valve if pressure exceeds 80 PSI. Replace aging supply lines, especially rubber washing machine hoses, with braided stainless steel. Finally, learn where the shutoff valve is ahead of time.
