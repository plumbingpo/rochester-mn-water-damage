Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Rochester: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Rochester finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Wet Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Damaged Flooring

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Rotted Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

To get at wet cavities we open only specific spots, take out unsalvageable cabinet parts, drywall or insulation, and seal anything moldy in bags so its spores stay out of the rest of the home.

3

Structural Drying

Drying equipment stays in place, typically for 3 to 5 days, until moisture in the framing and subfloor is back to normal. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. That paperwork proves the area was dry ahead of repairs and can support a claim.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • The type of flooring and finishes involved, since some dry in place and others do not.
  • Whether the damage stayed in one room or spread to others and to the level below.
  • How long the pipe leaked and how far the moisture spread from it.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
