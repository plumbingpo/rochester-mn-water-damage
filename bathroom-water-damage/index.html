Bathroom Water Damage Help in Rochester

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Rochester, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Warning Signs

  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.
