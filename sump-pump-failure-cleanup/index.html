Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Rochester

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelySee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Confirm

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Low Electrical Outlets

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.
