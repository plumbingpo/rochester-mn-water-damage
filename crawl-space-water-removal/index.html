Crawl Space Water Removal in Rochester

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Warning Signs

  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors

Our Process

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Settling Under the House

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Water That Is Not Clean

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.
