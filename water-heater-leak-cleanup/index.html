Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Rochester

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Rochester crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
  • Whether the platform or subfloor under the tank has to be rebuilt before a new heater goes in.
  • Where the heater sits, with attic and interior closet leaks costing more than garage leaks.
  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Closet Walls

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Remove Standing Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
